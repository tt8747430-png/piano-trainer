Labeling Chords & Choosing Scales
A major topic in this lesson is how jazz players label chords and why certain scales are paired with specific dominant sounds.
A useful rule of thumb:
Always label the 7th chord
Assume natural tensions
Explicitly label only the altered tensions
For example, a G7 already implies a natural 9 and 13—putting those notes together gives you G Mixolydian. This approach avoids confusing symbols like G9 or G13 and reflects how real players think about harmony.
From there, we look at why:
7♯5 chords are commonly paired with the whole-tone scale, even if the full scale isn’t spelled out in the symbol
7♭9 chords behave differently depending on whether they resolve to a major or minor chord

The half–whole (symmetric diminished) scale emerges naturally when combining altered tensions on dominant chords
These choices aren’t arbitrary—they come from experience, voice-leading, and avoiding harmonic clashes.
