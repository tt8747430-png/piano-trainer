## Description

Warren introduces the lesson on gospel piano, explaining that the focus will be on blues scales—specifically minor blues, major blues, and minor pentatonic scales, which are important for gospel musicians.
The minor blues scale is derived from the natural minor scale, with a specific formula (1, flat 3, 4, sharp 4, 5, flat 7) applied to create a bluesy sound often used in gospel music.
The major blues scale is introduced, which is based on the C major scale with a slight alteration (1, 2, flat 3, 3, 5, 6). This scale shares similarities with the major pentatonic scale but includes a blue note (flat 3).
The minor pentatonic scale is discussed as a variation of the major pentatonic scale, but starting from the sixth degree (1, flat 3, 4, 5, flat 7), and its close relationship to the blues scales is explained.
Warren emphasizes experimenting with these scales over different chords and progressions to develop improvisation skills, suggesting that understanding "wrong" notes is a key part of learning and refining one's playing.
