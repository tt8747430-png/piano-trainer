THE SHORT VERSION

I. Know which kind you are doing. Offense is learning a new piece: slow enough to see every note. Defense is when someone is relying on you: keep the music going. 
II. Before a single note, read the clefs, key, time signature and register. 
III. Keep your eyes a measure or two ahead of your hands. If your eyes can't take in every note, the tempo is too fast. 
IV. Read every chord from the bottom up. The bass gives you the harmony. 
V. On defense, let some of the right hand go first. Keep a solid bass line, the singer's cues and the right octave. 
VI. The only job is the next beat. 
VII. Practice with things that won't wait for you: Music Minus One, play-along apps, a friend and a stack of duets.
