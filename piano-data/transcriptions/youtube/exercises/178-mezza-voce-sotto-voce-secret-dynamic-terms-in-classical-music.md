## Description

Ever wonder why Chopin wrote mezza voce instead of mezzo-forte, or sotto voce instead of pianissimo?

In this Short, we look at alternative dynamic markings in Chopin’s Nocturne in C minor, Op. 48 No. 1. Rather than sticking strictly to volume levels like p or f, composers often used descriptive vocal terminology to give performers more leeway and subtle control over tone quality and color.

Mezza voce (m.v.) – "Half voice," calling for a warm, restrained, moderate volume similar to mezzo-forte.

Sotto voce – "Under the voice," indicating a soft, muffled, and intimate whisper-like tone.
