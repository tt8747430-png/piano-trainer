-- glossary --
1. diatonic - describes a note in the scale on which you're basing your composition. for example, a piece in E minor is based on the E minor scale, so notes like F# and B are diatonic while others like G# and Db are not
2. chromaticism - the use of notes not native to your key (i.e. non-diatonic). for example, if you are in the key of C major and you use the note Ab somewhere, that is chromaticism.
3. modality - the use of scales outside the typical major and minor ones. for instance lydian mode is the major scale with a raised 4th, and there are many others that composers like to play with often
4. pandiatonicism - using all the diatonic notes without really caring about functional harmony (chord conventions like dominant resolving to tonic, etc.)
5. atonality or "twelve-tone technique" - organizing all 12 notes of the chromatic scale into tone rows where each pitch is used exactly once. these tone rows can be transformed by rhythmic variation, retrograde, inversion, transposition, etc. and form the basis of an atonal composition. This music is called "atonal" because there is no functional tonal center.
6. polytonality - play in two or more keys at the same time
