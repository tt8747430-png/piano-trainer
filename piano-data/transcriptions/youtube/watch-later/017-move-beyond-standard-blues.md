## Description

Want to move beyond standard blues and make your blues piano playing more creative, expressive and versatile? Whether you're playing solo blues piano or performing with a band, understanding how to adapt your technique, chords and improvisation can completely transform your sound!

In this practical, step-by-step tutorial, Tom Donald explores two very different approaches to how to play blues: playing as a solo pianist and playing alongside other musicians.

🎹 Playing Solo Blues Piano – You Are the Entire Band!

When you're playing solo, you're responsible for everything: the bass, rhythm, harmony and improvisation. This makes hand coordination and independence essential.

Your left hand needs to maintain a steady rhythmic and harmonic foundation, often following the familiar 12 bar blues structure, while your right hand is free to explore creative blues riffs, melodic variations and improvisation.

Tom demonstrates two approaches to blues scale piano improvisation:
Using one blues scale across the entire 12 bar blues progression, creating freedom and continuity in your improvisation.

Following the chord changes, adapting your melodic ideas and note choices to reflect the underlying harmony for a more colourful and expressive sound.

🎹 Playing Blues With a Band – A Completely Different Approach!

When you're playing with other musicians, your role as a pianist changes dramatically. With a bass player already providing the low-end foundation, there's no need to duplicate the bass line!

Instead, Tom shows how to use rootless voicings and carefully chosen piano chords to create rich, interesting harmonies while leaving space for the other instruments.

Understanding how to adapt your chords, rhythmic patterns and how to improvise on piano to the musical setting is an essential skill for any blues or jazz pianist.

🎶 Putting It All Together – Miles Davis' Freddie Freeloader

To bring these ideas into a real musical context, Tom finishes the tutorial with Miles Davis' Freddie Freeloader.
