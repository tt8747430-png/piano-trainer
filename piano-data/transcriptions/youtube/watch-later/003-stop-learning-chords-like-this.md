## Description

🎹 Stop Learning Chords Like This!
Are you spending hours learning chords, memorising theory and chord shapes, but still struggling to make your piano playing sound fluent and musical?

After more than 20 years of teaching, Tom Donald has seen one mistake repeatedly: pianists try to learn as much chord theory as possible first, hoping they’ll eventually be able to apply it to real music. But knowing more chords doesn’t automatically make you better at how to play chords.

In this tutorial, Tom explains why the real goal of chord practice should be continuity and musicality.

Using several simple but highly effective piano chord progressions, Tom demonstrates how to build a strong foundation by connecting chords smoothly, keeping the music moving and developing the physical confidence to play without hesitation.

You’ll also explore chord inversions and how to play inversions without overcomplicating them with unnecessary theory. Instead, Tom focuses on practical shapes, efficient movement and repetition so that the connections between chords gradually become part of your muscle memory.

Tom then introduces a more funky, jazzy chord progression to show that even more sophisticated harmony can become manageable when you understand voice leading. Rather than thinking about every chord as an isolated theoretical problem, you learn to feel how one shape moves naturally into the next — creating better groove, continuity and musicality.

And there’s one particularly important piano practice principle in this lesson: don’t become dependent on the screen.
Take the notes and ideas you need from the tutorial, then switch the screen off, go to the piano and figure them out for yourself. That process is essential for developing muscle memory and turning information into something you can actually play fluently.

If you want better learning strategies for chords, more effective piano practice, and practical piano tips that help you become a more confident and musical player, this tutorial will give you a completely different approach to learning harmony.
