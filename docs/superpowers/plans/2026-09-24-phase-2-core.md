### Deliberate refinements of the spec

Each is already written into the spec (§ in brackets) and the glossary, so the spec stays the one description of the
design. The owner reviews them there.

1. **Transposition keeps letters** (§4.1). Moving by semitones and then choosing sharps or flats loses the letter,
   which is the failure §4.1's own rule forbids. The kernel has `transposeNote(note, fromTonic, toTonic)`: the
   interval between the old tonic and the note, applied to the new tonic. A root that would need a double accidental
   gets its plain spelling. Chord tones and slash basses are then spelled from the root, so `D#/G` in G♯ minor reads
   `D#/F𝄪`, and in A♯ minor `E#/G𝄪`.
2. **The melody toggle is an arrange option** (`doubleMelody`, §4.3–4.4), not a schedule option: it changes which
   notes exist and therefore the beat groups Step mode walks through.
3. **Progressions are packed into real bars** (§4.2). Legacy made each progression chord its own "bar" (a 16-beat
   bar in the blues). The chart now has 4-beat bars, a chord longer than a bar is tied across bar lines, so the
   12-bar blues has 12 bars. The sound is unchanged: patterns already restarted every bar.
4. **Blues roots on the black keys change** (§4.1). Legacy spelled every blues note from a flat-name table, with roots
   D♭, G♭ and A♭. Spelled by letter steps from those roots the scale needs F♭ and C♭ (D♭ blues: D♭ F♭ G♭ G A♭ C♭).
   So the blues is spelled from its minor-key root, as the other minor kinds are: C♯, F♯ and G♯ (E♭ and B♭ stay).
   C♯ blues reads C♯ E F♯ G G♯ B. The blue note is ♭5, or ♯4 when that needs fewer accidentals (E♭ blues: `A`, not
   `B𝄫`). The §12 parity check expects these three roots to read differently from legacy.
5. **`C°` means the diminished triad only** (§4.1). Legacy listed it under both `dim` and `°7`; one symbol, one chord.
6. **`arrange` takes a pattern, the chart's method patterns and per-hand figures** (§4.3), instead of
   `Pattern | { rh: Pattern; lh: Pattern } | 'from-chart'`. The Player's per-hand choice is a right-hand and a
   left-hand **figure** (legacy `RHP`/`LHP`), not two patterns. `'from-chart'` cannot work inside `arrangement`,
   which may not import the method table from `entities/pattern`, so the caller passes `methods`.
7. **One `Tone` type for chord tones and scale notes** (§4.1). Both are a spelled note measured from a root with its
   role and degree label; one type lets the keyboard mark either with the same code.
8. **`practised` replaces `lastPractised`** (§5–6). My gaps (§4.6 ③) needs every piece the learner has opened, and
   one `lastPractised` field holds only the latest. `practised: Record<PieceId, isoDate>` records each piece's last
   opening, and the last-practised piece is the latest entry. `pt-progress` starts at version 1 with this shape.
9. **Credits are structured** (§4.2, §8). Legacy credit strings carry English labels ("Words and music:", "Author
   unknown"). A credit is `{ role, names }`: the names stay as printed and the role label is an interface string in
   both languages.
10. **`usePractice(performance, setup)` reads `useServices()`** (§4.5) instead of taking the services, as CODE_STYLE
    §8 asks. Recording that a piece was opened is its own command, `features/record-practised` (§3).
11. **Automatic marking happens once** (§4.6 ②). A chord or scale step is marked learned by the answer that makes all
    its skills Known. A step the learner unmarks stays unmarked until one of its skills slips and comes back.

## Review Focus

1. **Transposing a piece to an awkward key** (G♯ minor chart to A♯ minor, slash chords whose bass is a chord tone,
   a chromatic root in a sharp key) → every root has at most one sharp or flat, every chord tone is spelled from its
   root, and nothing throws. Pinned by: Task 2 (`transposeNote`), Task 8 (transposition tests), Task 13 (every piece
   in all 12 keys, asserting root accidentals).
2. **Bars that are not the meter's length** (a pickup bar `Fm@1`, 2/4 bars inside 4/4, a bar longer than the meter,
   half-beat chords) → the engine places every note inside its chord's span and never loops forever. Pinned by:
   Task 8 (long bar, short chord offset), Task 13 (catalog invariants on every piece).
3. **Saved data from an older version, corrupt, or hand-edited** (settings v1 without practice/quiz fields, an empty
   family list, unknown skills, more than 5 answers, bad dates) → valid fields kept, the rest defaulted or trimmed,
   never a crash. Pinned by: Task 15 and Task 16 store tests.
4. **A melody-based pattern on a piece with no melody**, a melody right-hand figure chosen for such a piece, or the
   melody toggle on a piece without one → the whole pattern falls back to `r4` (both hands, and the chord records
   `r4`), a chosen melody figure falls back to the `r4` figure, and nothing throws. Pinned by: Task 8 (fallback),
   Task 13 (every piece × every pattern × 12 keys).
5. **MIDI input in real life** (note-on with velocity 0 as note-off, several devices, a device unplugged, the same
   note in another octave, keys pressed after the beat is complete) → counts once, any octave, status follows the
   devices. Pinned by: Task 19 (message parsing, adapter), Task 21 (Your turn), Task 22 (the hook).
6. **A learner who unmarks a step the quiz marked** → it stays unmarked while its skills stay Known. Pinned by:
   Task 15 (`stepCompletedBy`), Task 17 (`recordAnswer`).

suffix. Suffixes are compared after normalising `♭ → b`, `♯ → #`, `− → -`, against a map built once from every
quality's `qualitySpellings`.
