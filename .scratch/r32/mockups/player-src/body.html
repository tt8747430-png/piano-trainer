The Player · Round 32

The Player on the iPhone 17 Pro as the installed app and on the MacBook Air 15″ in Safari. This round answers four requests: proper pedals beneath the keyboard, no space reserved above it, Settings ordered by what you change most with every option explained in a line, and the remaining parts of the Round 31 design. Playback on dense scores is already up to four times smoother (Chopin’s Fantaisie-Impromptu: 2.25 s → 0.6 s of blocked time per 10 s).

Each screen has Approve, Revise or Remove and a note; your answers stay in this browser.

1

The score, the keyboard and the transport, with nothing in between

Where: the Player, /play/hot-cross-buns.

What changes: the keyboard sits directly beneath the score. The band above it is gone; its controls move to the pedal rail beneath the keys. The tip no longer takes a row: it floats on the transport’s top edge until you dismiss it. MIDI appears in the bar only while a keyboard is connected. The tempo reads as a metronome mark. On a laptop the transport and the song tools share one row.

Recommendation: as drawn. MuseScore docks its keyboard straight under the score and Songscription shows the tempo as a metronome mark; both keep the music as large as the screen allows.
2

Pedals beneath the keyboard, as on a piano

Where: every on-screen piano — the Player, the score editor, Chords, Scales and the pinned keyboard.

What changes: the pedals stand centred beneath the keys on the piano’s rail — soft, sostenuto and sustain, or the sustain alone. A pedal sinks and glows while it is down, whether the score, a MIDI pedal, the space bar or your finger holds it. Hold it to press; tap the sustain to latch it, tap again to release. The chord you hold is named at the rail’s start; key size and the piano’s settings sit at its end.

Recommendation: A, centred beneath the keys. It reads as the instrument (Pianoteq, any upright or grand) and keeps the keys the full width of the screen; B narrows the keys by a column on a phone.
3

Settings ordered by relevance, each option explained

Where: the Player’s ⚙; the On-screen piano page in Settings, opened from it and from every piano rail’s ⚙.

What changes: the pages appear in the order a pianist reaches for them — Score view, Playback, Staves, Accompaniment, then a link to the On-screen piano page, the one home of every keyboard’s settings — each with one line on what it controls and its current choice. Every option on a page carries a one-line description; pictures show the layouts, key sizes, labels and pedals.
