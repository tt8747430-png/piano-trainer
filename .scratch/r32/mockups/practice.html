Practice: what to do next, then by relevance

Where: /practice.

What changes: a purpose line under the title; Up next replaces Continue: up to three cards your own record proves are next (skills due for review, the exercise to resume at its tempo, the page you last worked in), each saying why. Then Theory, Playing and Training; the Tools last, as four compact tiles showing their current value. A new learner sees “Start here”.

Recommendation: as drawn. Yousician, Simply Piano and Flowkey open on the next step with one action; musictheory.net orders Lessons, Exercises, Tools. Continue is removed: it repeated rows listed below it and missed pages opened from Search or a link.
2

Metronome: every setting in place, as Chordify

Where: /practice/metronome. The gear and /practice/metronome/settings go.

What changes: Time signature, Sound and Subdivision stand under the beats; the speed trainer and silent bars sit below as Practice aids, each a switch that opens its rows in place. On a laptop the controls stand beside the face.

3

Tuner and Circle of fifths

Where: /practice/tuner, /practice/circle-of-fifths. The Tuner’s settings page goes.

What changes: the reference pitch (A4 = 440 Hz) is a stepper under the readout, its only setting, in place. The Circle of fifths is unchanged and drawn for context.

4

Chord finder: a clear empty state and clear settings

Where: /practice/chord-finder and its settings.

What changes: empty, the stage says what to do in one line and offers three example chords to try; the empty staff waits until there is a chord. The key picker reads Spelled in, with an ⓘ saying why. Its settings are grouped and named for what they do.

5

The header’s Tools: a real menu, in place

Where: the grid button in the bar of Chords, Scales and keys, Intervals, the Chord player and a lesson.

What changes: the Metronome runs live in it; the Tuner listens in it, its readout opening under its switch; the Chord finder and the Circle of fifths open their own pages. “All tools”, which sent you to Practice, goes.

6

Selection sheets that say what is chosen and why

Where: Quiz › a drill › Custom › Chord qualities, Intervals, Scales.

What changes: a summary first (“8 of 27 included · Level 2 set”), with Select all, Clear and Level set; each group counts what it includes and has All or None; each row has a check box at its start and an example at its end (Cm7, m3 · 3 semitones).

7

Chord player: simpler, and drag and drop that works

Where: /practice/chord-player.

What changes: a section is a row of equal chord tiles (numeral under the symbol, the beats as dots). Tap a tile for its chord sheet (symbol, length, bass, passing chords). Hold a tile (or drag it with the mouse) to move it: the tile lifts, the others make room, it settles where you drop it. The resize edge and the double tap go. Sections move by their handle.

Recommendation: as drawn. It follows memory-palaces’ engine (one drag context, a hold of a quarter second, the dropped order held on screen) and Apple’s rule that a tap opens and a hold lifts.
8

Libraries you can arrange, built-in books included

Where: /practice/exercises, /practice/accompaniment, /practice/progressions.

What changes: Edit (the pencil beside the title) gives every place and row a handle; each place renames, hides or (your folders) deletes; New folder. Every row’s ⋯ has Favourite, Rename, Move to, Hide. A renamed built-in keeps a Restore original name. Accompaniment holds patterns only: each method book links to its Course in Learn and its Songs in Songs.
