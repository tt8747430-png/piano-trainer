1

Edit sits beside the title, on every screen

Where: every screen's bar (the song, a folder, a lesson, a course, a progression, a pattern, Learn).

What changes: the pencil moves from the far right of the bar to right after the title, so it reads "edit this". The bar's other buttons keep their order: tools, Settings, More. While editing, Done stands in the pencil's place, so the control never moves between modes, and the buttons that do not apply while editing (a song's Learned and ⚙, a folder's Select and ⚙) step away until Done, so the title keeps its room.

Recommendation: as drawn. It is your ruling ("why the edit button is not beside the title"), and Notion, Craft and Apple Notes put a rename or edit control on the title itself.
2

Songs, the place

Where: /songs, My songs.

What changes: the title "Songs" is back in the bar; My songs · Explore becomes a small switch under it, its own width. The phone shows Continue (last played), Folders, Shared with me, then All songs. The laptop fills the screen: the library tree on the left (as Mindscape and Apple Music do), the chosen list on the right as a table you can sort by any column.

Recommendation: A, the two panes. The laptop screen is full, folders are one click away and are drop targets for dragging songs, and the phone shows the same parts as sections, in the tree's order. B keeps the phone's sections but loses sorting by column and makes every folder a page away.
3

A folder

Where: /songs/folders/hymns.

What changes: the folder's name has ✎ beside it. Tapping it makes the name a field in place, with its picture and colour under it; Done ends it. A count line under the bar ("5 songs · 2 learned"), and when shared, which group has it. Songs are rows: title, who wrote it, key, level and the Learned tick.

Recommendation: as drawn. Mindscape and Apple Notes rename a folder where its name reads; the delete dialog names what is lost, including the groups that lose the folder.
4

A song's page

Where: /songs/amazing.

What changes: Learned moves to the bar's right as a toggle and Play floats (your earlier ruling). ✎ beside the title puts the page in Edit right where it reads: the title, About, Level, Genre, Language, Tags and the cover become fields. The laptop uses two columns: the song on the left, its first lines of music, sections and chords on the right.

Recommendation: as drawn. It keeps your rulings (Play floats, Learned at the header's right, Edit beside the title) and Chordify's song page order: the song, its chords, then its parts. "Your practice" on the laptop shows what the app already records for the song: plays, best tempo and takes.
5

Score details and song details

Where: the song's page (its Edit) and the score editor (Score › Title and credits).

What changes: two kinds of details, each with one home. Score details are what prints on the page and are edited on the score. Song details are about the song in your library, never printed, and edited on the song's page. Music and Words today exist twice (the song's facts and the score's Composer and Lyricist); they become the score's alone, and the song's page reads them from it.

6

Groups and private folders

Where: Songs › Groups (/songs/groups), a group's page, a folder's Settings › Share with a group, and Songs › Shared with me.

What changes: with an account you make a group (a class, a choir, a band), invite people by a link, and share any folder with it, each group able to view or to edit. Members find it under Shared with me; a view-only member can Duplicate a song into their own songs. Nothing is public: only the group's members see it.

Recommendation: invite by link (WhatsApp, Telegram, a class email) rather than by typing addresses: it needs no search over other people's accounts, and the owner can switch the link off.
7

Explore

Where: /songs, Explore.

What changes: the same small switch; categories as chips; shelves that fill the laptop (five songs a row) with their ratings and authors; offline it says so and offers My songs.

Recommendation: as drawn. Noji's public library uses the same shelf, rating and author card; offline, Explore points back to what works.
