1

One bar, one dock, nothing floating over the score

Where: the Player, /play/a-song.

What changes: the pill over the score’s title and the transport over its staves go. The bar keeps ✕, the title with ✎, Piano keys and ⚙ (the Tools grid goes). The dock at the foot holds the transport (Loop, Restart, ‹ ▶ ›, Tempo, Click) and the song tools (View, Key, Version, Mode, More). Every Tools-sheet row has one home now: its act in More, its setting in ⚙.

Recommendation: A, the song tools in the dock’s second row. Chordify’s phone app keeps its song tools at the foot with the play bar; the top stays the song’s name, and the score’s first line is never covered.
2

The piano and a real sustain pedal

Where: the Player with Piano keys on (and the score editor, which shares the piano).

What changes: no grey box and no row of buttons over the keys: a slim strip with the chord, − +, and ⚙ to the piano’s settings. At the strip’s end, a drawn sustain pedal goes down with the score’s pedal while playing; hold it, or tap it to lock it, when you play the keys yourself. Stopped, it lifts and no key stays lit.

3

Settings as pages

Where: the Player’s ⚙.

What changes: one list of pages, each with Back: Staves, Accompaniment, Playback, Display, On-screen piano (in this sheet now, not the app’s Settings), Tool bar. Each staff has its own page (Show, Play, Volume, hand). Swing and the pedal live in Playback; the sheet’s layout lives in Display alone.

4

Version, Transpose, More, Tempo, a tip

Where: the dock’s Version, Key, More and Tempo.

What changes: Version says what each one is in plain words, the arrangement chosen at its head. Transpose shows the first line in the new key before you apply it, as the editor does. More holds acts only. A tip shows once, in a line.
