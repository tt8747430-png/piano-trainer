# Hooktheory: key cheat sheet and music reference

Sources, read 2026-11-06: https://www.hooktheory.com/cheat-sheet/key/c/major (captured fully loaded:
`hooktheory-laptop.png`, `hooktheory-phone.png`) and https://www.hooktheory.com/support/musicreference with its concept
pages. Their texts, song data and colours' exact values are theirs: we take the ideas and write our own.

## The key cheat sheet (one page per key and mode)

- Left rail: the relative keys (C major: A minor, G Mixolydian, D Dorian, F Lydian, E Phrygian, B Locrian) and every
  cheat sheet by mode (major, minor, Dorian, Mixolydian, Lydian, Phrygian, Locrian), twelve keys each.
- The scale on a piano, each degree in its own colour (1 red, 2 orange, 3 yellow, 4 green, 5 blue, 6 purple, 7 pink),
  with Play; the key signature beside it.
- About the key: its signature, its notes, how common it is, the three major chords.
- **The 21 most-used chords in the key**, ranked from 82,000 analysed songs, each a tile coloured by its degree with its
  Roman numeral under it: the diatonic chords, then inversions (C/E I6, G/B V6), sevenths, V/V, V/vi, sus4, and borrowed
  ♭VII, ♭VI (hatched as borrowed). A tap plays it and shows it on a piano or a guitar.
- **Popular progressions in the key** (I–V–vi–IV, I–vi–IV–V, I–IV–ii–V…), each a coloured strip with Play, a small
  piano following along, MIDI download, Open in Hookpad.
- Songs in the key, from their database, each opening its TheoryTab (chords and melody synced to the video).

## The music reference (one page per concept)

Fundamentals: scale, interval. Functional harmony: harmony, key, relative notation, scale degree, Roman numerals.
Basic chords: basic chord, chord quality, voicing, cadence. Melody: meter, stable and unstable degrees. Advanced chords:
inversions, sevenths, secondary (applied) chords, suspended, add, borrowed, tritone substitution, altered. Advanced
harmony: transposition, modal mixture, modes, (non-)diatonic, stepwise bass, parallel and relative.

Each page: a short, plain explanation in the key of C, Roman numerals inline, an interactive example from a real song
with the non-diatonic note coloured, then a list of songs that use it, each opening its analysis.

## Against ours

| Hooktheory | Ours |
| --- | --- |
| A page per key and mode | Scales and keys (`/practice/scales`, `widgets/scale-explorer`, `KeyChords`), Circle of fifths |
| Chords coloured by scale degree, everywhere (scale, tiles, progressions, piano keys) | no degree colours |
| The key's most-used chords, beyond the diatonic ones (inversions, V/V, ♭VII, sus4), ranked | the diatonic chords |
| Popular progressions per key, playable with a piano following, MIDI out | Progressions library, not per key |
| Relative keys and modes linked from each key | to check in the scale explorer |
| Songs in this key | our Songs carry a key; no "songs in this key" link |
| A concept reference, each term its own short page with a playable example and songs using it | the same topics are taught inside lessons (secondary dominants, borrowed chords, tritone substitution, sus, inversions, cadences); no reference or glossary to jump to |

## Worth porting, in order

1. **Colour by scale degree** as one shared palette (our own colours), used by the scale explorer, chord tiles,
   progressions, the Player's chord names and the on-screen piano; a setting to turn it off.
2. **The key page** in Scales and keys: relative keys and modes as links, the key's common chords beyond the diatonic
   (inversions, V/V, V/vi, ♭VII, ♭VI, sus4, sevenths) with Roman numerals, borrowed ones marked, each playable.
3. **Common progressions per key**, from our Progressions library transposed to the key, playable with the piano
   following, opening the progression's page.
4. **Songs in this key** from Songs and Explore.
5. **A theory reference**: one short page per concept (the list above), each with a playable example in our own
   notation and links to the lessons that teach it and the songs that use it; terms in lessons and on the song page
   link to it, and Search finds it. EN and RU, written by us.
