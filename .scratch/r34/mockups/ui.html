UI sketches · Round 34

What the references taught us (Piano Forte, Hookpad, StemDeck, Songscription, PianoBooster), drawn on our own screens. Each question shows today beside the change where it helps (A today, B the change), on the iPhone 17 Pro and the MacBook Air with Safari, with real songs and the exact labels.

Each screen has Approve, Revise or Remove and a note; your answers stay in this browser.

1

Shapes beside every judged note, not colour alone

Where: the Player's score after a Sight-read or Practice run, and its summary.

What changes: today a note you played is only tinted, and early and late share the same honey. Each judged note now gets a small shape beside its head: ● on time, ◂ early, ▸ late, ○ missed, × wrong. The summary carries the same legend, so the colour is never the only cue.

Recommendation: B, after Piano Forte's Word And Shape Rule. It is what DESIGN.md's Second Cue Rule asks for.
2

The left hand outlined, the right hand filled

Where: every on-screen keyboard showing hands (Player, editor, lessons).

What changes: the right hand stays a solid brick key; the left hand becomes a lilac wash with a lilac edge. The hands differ by fill as well as colour, so they still read apart for colour-blind players.

Recommendation: B, Piano Forte's hand fills.
3

A cover engraved from the song's first bars

Where: Songs (rows and cards), the song page, Explore.

What changes: the big key letter on yellow becomes the song's first two bars, engraved small on paper. A picture you chose still wins over it. A song with no notes yet shows an empty staff with its key and time signature.

Recommendation: B, Piano Forte's covers. Songs become recognisable at a glance.
4

The song page: your practice and one next step

Where: a song's page (/songs/$id), phone and laptop.

What changes: on a phone the cover, title and Play come first, then your practice: last run, best run and a bar-by-bar strip, then one next step as a quiet button, so Play stays the only honey. Details fold below. On a laptop the empty right column holds the same practice card. “Change cover” moves into the cover's own menu, so it no longer overlaps the favourite.

Recommendation: as drawn. The next step follows Piano Forte's pace rules: loop the weakest bars at 70%, then 75% → 100%, then hands together.
5

Timing calibration

Where: Settings › On-screen piano and MIDI › Calibrate timing.

What changes: play middle C along with 16 clicks. The app measures how late your setup sounds (Bluetooth speakers, USB keyboards) and corrects every judgement by it. It needs 8 good taps; Try again otherwise.

Recommendation: as drawn, Piano Forte's calibration (the median of your taps, to 5 ms).
6

Editor on a phone: one target at the start of a line

Where: the score editor (/edit/$id) on a phone.

What changes: the clef, key and time signature at a line's start are too narrow to tap (7–24 px). On a phone they become one target that opens Clef · Key signature · Time signature; each opens what it opens today. The note-entry panel docks above the keys instead of floating over the score, and the view choice (Page, Rows, Horizontal) moves into the gear. A staff added over the piano is named by its part (Melody), not “Staff 1”.

Recommendation: as drawn, Flat for iPhone's line-start menu.
7

Scales: the music first, one layer of labels

Where: Practice › Scales and keys (/practice/scales), laptop and phone.

What changes: the grid of eight controls moves into the gear; the heading keeps the key and scale. Above the staff, one choice says what the staff shows: Fingers · Degrees · Steps. With Steps, W and H sit between the notes, never on them, so nothing collides.

Recommendation: as drawn. Music first, as Hooktheory's key pages.
8

Progressions as chord tiles coloured by role

Where: Practice › Progressions (list and a progression's page).

What changes: each row shows its chords as small tiles tinted by role: home green, away sand, tension lilac. They replace the identical list icon and the plain “I–V–vi–IV”. On a progression's page the tiles are large and playable, and the one sounding is ringed in ink. Absolute or relative names are one switch in place.

Recommendation: B. Hookpad's tiles, but coloured by role, as our Palette Law asks, not by scale degree.
9

Songs: a Trash, and rows with one action

Where: Songs (/songs).

What changes: Delete moves a song to Trash, and nothing is gone until you empty it. The archive button becomes a menu: Archive · Trash. Folder rows drop the chevron: tap the row to open it, and its ⋯ button for the menu.

Recommendation: as drawn, StemDeck's Trash. Your decision: keep Archive beside Trash (drawn), or merge them.
10

The recording follows Transpose and slows cleanly

Where: the Player with a song's recording (Original).

What changes: today Transpose silences the recording. It now shifts the recording's pitch too, and slowing down keeps the pitch (0.5–1×, without the chipmunk or the drag). The waveform shows the recording's beat grid, found once, which you can nudge, so the click and the score's cursor stay with it.

Recommendation: as drawn, with Signalsmith Stretch (MIT) and web-audio-beat-detector (MIT).
